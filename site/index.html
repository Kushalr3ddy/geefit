What Global Expo Events is

One programme, seven stops.

Most Indian trade shows happen once, in one hall, in one city — which means the supplier in Coimbatore and the student in Kolkata never make it. Global Expo Events takes the same exhibition floor and the same visiting delegations on the road: twenty-one days, seven cities, so the sector comes to the country instead of the country coming to Delhi.

Each stop runs the full five tracks — education, employment, finance, industry and technology — so a single visit can mean a course prospectus, a job interview, a distributor conversation and an investor meeting in one afternoon.

Why it matters

Aerospace is being built in India. We want it built everywhere in India.

India is assembling aircraft, launching satellites and manufacturing components at a scale it never has before. The bottleneck isn't ambition — it's contact. Suppliers who could qualify never meet the primes. Graduates trained for the work never meet the employers. Investors never see the shop floor.

An expo that travels fixes a small, specific part of that: it puts the primes, the suppliers, the institutions and the graduates in the same hall, seven times over. That's the whole idea, and it's enough.

Our inspiration

What we've set ourselves to be judged by.

Sir M. Visvesvaraya built dams, cities and colleges in Karnataka on the belief that industry is what turns engineering into livelihoods. That is the standard we've set ourselves: an expo is only worth holding if somebody leaves it with a contract, a job or a place on a course. We keep his portrait on our wall as a reminder that the measure is what got built, not what got announced.
